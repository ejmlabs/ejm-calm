- Spout was over-bright (additive sprites above the tone-map white point, plus a matching blob in the water mirror). This pass makes it a short translucent mist column on layer 1 so the Water mirror skips it. Monitor it on a device.
- Cloudflare zone injects its Web Analytics beacon / inline script. The strict CSP blocks it (harmless console errors). Disable RUM/beacon injection on the zone if those errors should go away.
- Horizon seam under ACES can still show a thin bright water/sky line.
- Desktop seed 1234 still frames the whale very close, so the head reads as a rounded end-on mass. Pull the default camera back slightly if that crop should show the full side profile.
- Land is shaded per fragment from the live sun direction, haze colours and a day brightness, so it follows the day cycle; the vertex colours carry the palette only.
- Mobile breach/spout camera still does not reliably fill 35–50% of screen width; needs a bbox-centred look and a screen-width check.
- Land haze colours are the sky straight away from the sun, 0.3 to 16 degrees above the horizon, measured per preset by `tools/haze-measure.ts`; re-run it and paste the arrays whenever a sky preset or the sky shader changes. Between two presets the haze is a blend of two measured colours, which can sit up to about 40 sRGB levels off the sky while the sun moves fast (afternoon to golden, dusk to night). The night haze is a faint navy set by hand; the measured night sky is black.
- Shallows fade out between 150 and 900 m and at grazing angles, so from boat height the turquoise shows only near a shore; a higher camera shows more.
- Sail billow is visible up close; at distance a tall ship can still read as one pale panel.
- Saved camera pitch/distance can override the portrait defaults after a refresh.
- Real-device figures are still owed for the migration: the p99 stream frame (spec: under 3 ms on a phone-class device), the environment map refresh frame (spec: at most 8 ms over the average on the desktop profile), and frame time at cruise on a phone. Software GL numbers are recorded in `openspec/changes/add-ocean-migration/tasks.md` and are not those.
- Forward progress on the planet layout: over an hour on seeds 1 to 50 and the test set net over path is 0.83 to 0.99 (median 0.95) and no ten minutes make under 7.2 km; seed 23 makes 1.3 km in its first five minutes (the opening's exit). `tools/seed-sweep.ts` is the gate.
- The continents are placed for the first 500 km of the lane (about seven hours); beyond that they fall as they may, so longer coastal or open stretches are possible. The whale wanders up to about 9 km off the lane's centreline over an hour, beyond the 6 km kept open; it then meets ordinary coasts, which the planner handles in the sweep.
- Coast in view (natural land within 6 km) is 28 percent of the first hour on average but 10 to 68 percent by seed; four seeds of 56 spend over half their first hour beside a continent.
- At cruise the follower camera trails the whale by about 2 v / 2.6 m on top of the 25 percent pull-back, so the whale sits further off than the pull-back alone says (about 40 m at 28 m/s); the whale stays inside the middle 80 percent of the frame.
- Rocks and islets stand one per 2 km cell, only where the sea is open (no natural coast within a kilometre), so something passes every couple of minutes without narrowing a way through; they add land tiles with meshes (about 200 land tiles at once on seed 42, against about 70 before the change began).
- Distant ranges now shade from per-tile maps, but their skylines are still the mesh's straight segments (vertices 85 m apart on a 2 km tile). A finer grid for the coarse levels only, measured on a real GPU, would break the skyline too.
- First-frame time on the load-time profile is dominated by shader linking in software GL (about 1 to 3 s of it on this machine, on main as well), and wall-clock medians on a shared, busy machine swing by a second between runs of the same build. Real GPUs link far faster. The page links the materials first drawn after the opening frame by drawing them once before it; where the parallel shader compile extension exists (not in the software GL used here), three.js's `compileAsync` could do that off the main thread instead. The water mirror's variant of the land dissolve still links on first use (about 70 ms here).
- Land tiles cross-fade by sample coverage wherever the frame multisamples; the hatched dissolve remains only on CPU renderers (`plain` frame).
- Golden hour and dusk rely on a warm horizon belt in the sky shader (`BELT_TINT` and `BELT_FALLOFF` in `src/sky.ts`), because the sky model alone runs brown, olive, then blue away from a low sun. The tint is art direction, not physics; the sunward half of the sky is the model's own. Seeds that open at golden have the sun 1 to 2 degrees below the preset's, so their sky is a little darker and redder than the measured haze, and dusk is very dark when a seed's sun sits near 2 degrees.
- From the chase camera the whale's back can read glassy blue in clear afternoon light (the clear coat reflecting the sky); as on main.
- The HDR finish (4x multisampled half-float scene, bloom, grade, `src/finish.ts`) has no device figures yet: frame time on a phone and on a desktop GPU is owed. In software GL it took the desktop frame from a median of 417 to 1,167 ms (seed 42, a four-core container), which is why CPU renderers draw without it; that is not a device figure.
- GPUs without multisampled half-float targets draw the `multisampled` frame (antialiased, no bloom or grade). It was checked only in software GL with `?debug=1&frame=multisampled`; no such device was at hand.
- `package.json` overrides minimatch to 11.0.3 under the lint tools: eslint 10.13.0 and typescript-eslint 8.71.2 (the shared layer's pins) still ask for minimatch 10, which has a denial-of-service advisory fixed in 11.0.1. Lint passes and its ignore patterns match with 11. Remove the override when the shared layer moves to an eslint that takes minimatch 11 (ejmlabs/.github owns those pins).
